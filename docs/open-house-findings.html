What changed in mental health clinical trials from 2010 to 2025?

Long image

A

Answer

Trials moved toward common disorders, non-drug treatments and university sponsors. This comes from 14,724 trials on ClinicalTrials.gov.1

  1. Anxiety trials grew 5.7 times, but schizophrenia and bipolar trials grew only 1.2 times.1
  2. Behavioural trials grew 3.4 times and device trials 10 times, while drug trials stayed level.1
  3. Universities and hospitals now sponsor nearly 9 in 10 trials.1
  4. Canada has the second most trials after the United States.1

1

Which conditions grew fastest?

More common disorders got more new research. Severe mental illness got very little new growth. The result stays the same when the window ends in 2024.2

2

Which treatments do trials test now?

Drugs fell from 44% to 15% of treatment types. Brain stimulation (TMS, tDCS) and virtual reality drove the device growth.1

What else changed?

  • Psychedelic trials surged: 80% of the 165 psychedelic or MDMA trials started in 2021 or later.1
  • Industry sponsors 23% of recent psychedelic trials, against about 9% of all trials.
  • COVID-19 cut new trials in 7 of 8 groups in 2020. In 2021, all 8 groups rose.
  • Virtual reality grew from 0.5% to 5.2% of trials, the fastest of all title topics.
  • Machine learning predicted early stops only a little better than chance (ROC AUC 0.66).
6

What are the limits of this data?

Read the numbers with these limits

ClinicalTrials.gov is a US registry, so many trials from other continents are not in it. Total registration grew over these years, so compare groups with each other, not raw growth. The 8 condition groups come from my own keyword rules.
